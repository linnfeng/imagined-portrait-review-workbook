# Imagined portrait consent and review workbook

![AI Baby Generator logo](https://ai-baby-generator.org/logo.png)

Imagined baby portraits can be a playful creative exercise for adults who are curious about how a fictional child might look. They are not a way to predict a real child's features, health, ancestry, or future. A useful approach starts by setting that expectation, choosing photos responsibly, and treating every generated picture as an illustration rather than evidence about a family.

AI Baby Generator is a web tool described as creating imagined baby or child portraits from two adult photos, with choices for age, gender, and resemblance. If you explore the [ai baby generator](https://ai-baby-generator.org/), approach the result as a creative interpretation. I am associated with this project, and AI assisted in drafting this workbook. It explains general choices and limitations rather than reporting an independent test of the service. Check the current pricing and privacy information before using any image tool.

## Begin with a clear purpose

Decide what you want from the exercise before choosing images. You might want a private conversation starter, a fictional character reference, or an imaginative illustration for a personal project. Those goals suggest different decisions about framing, image storage, and sharing. A picture intended only for a short private conversation does not need to be posted publicly; a character concept may benefit from notes about lighting and expression instead of claims about inherited traits.

Tell everyone involved that the image is synthetic. A realistic portrait can be mistaken for a photograph when it is detached from its original context. A small caption such as “AI-generated imagined portrait” helps others understand what they are seeing. Keep the label with the image if you move it into a presentation or a message, rather than assuming everyone who sees it has heard the original explanation.

## Ask permission for source photos

Use photos of adults who have agreed to this particular use. Someone may be comfortable sharing a picture with you but not with uploading it to an image service or generating a fictional portrait from it. Explain the proposed use, the service involved, whether you plan to save the output, and whether anyone else will see it. Give the other person a real opportunity to decline without pressure.

Avoid uploading pictures of strangers, children, or people who cannot consent. If a photo contains additional faces in the background, choose a different image or crop it appropriately before proceeding. Permission is also relevant when someone else took the photograph: having access to a file does not necessarily grant permission to reuse it in every setting. When in doubt, choose a photo you have the right to use and for which the people pictured have agreed.

## Review the service's current privacy terms

Before uploading, read the current privacy information and any relevant terms on the service itself. Look for what happens to source images and generated results, how long they may be retained, whether they are used for model improvement, and what deletion options are offered. If the explanation is unclear, avoid assuming that a picture disappears automatically after a browser tab closes.

Consider the sensitivity of a face image even when it appears ordinary. Prefer the least revealing photo that is suitable for the activity. Remove identifying information from the image frame, such as an address, work badge, or document. Avoid sending a photo simply because it is convenient if it includes personal details unrelated to your goal. A private local folder with a clear deletion date can also make it easier to avoid keeping unnecessary copies.

## Pick source photos for clarity

When choosing an adult portrait, look for even lighting, a reasonably clear view of the face, and a natural expression. Strong backlighting, motion blur, heavy filters, or a face hidden behind accessories can make an image harder for a system to interpret consistently. A simple background makes it easier for a person to check what is actually included in the frame before upload.

Do not interpret these recommendations as a guarantee of output quality. Different services may process images differently, and a photo that looks clear to a human may still lead to unexpected results. Follow the current interface's requirements for format and size. If an image is rejected, check the displayed message and try a permitted file format or a different photo for which you have permission, rather than weakening security settings or bypassing a restriction.

## Make one change at a time

If the interface offers age, gender, or resemblance settings, choose values that match the intended fictional illustration. Record which choices you made if you want to compare outputs thoughtfully. Changing every setting at once makes it difficult to understand which choice produced a visible difference. A simple comparison might start with the same source images and vary only the age setting, subject to the service's current controls and terms.

Keep comparison modest. Repeated generation is not a scientific sampling process and does not reveal the probabilities of a real child's traits. Models can reflect patterns from training data and design choices that are not visible to the user. If several pictures share a feature, that repetition might say more about the system or the input images than about any actual family relationship.

## Examine the result as an illustration

Look at the overall expression, pose, lighting, and background first. Then inspect small details such as ears, fingers, clothing edges, and objects near the face. Generated images can contain inconsistencies even when the first impression looks convincing. If you intend to use an image in a creative project, these details can help you decide whether it needs further editing or should simply remain a private experiment.

Also consider whether the result introduces assumptions you did not intend. A model might associate age, appearance, clothing, or setting with stereotypes. There is no obligation to accept those associations. You can discard an image that makes someone uncomfortable, select another setting if available, or stop the exercise. Avoid describing a generated feature as a reliable inherited characteristic or as an indication of a real person's identity.

## Keep imagination separate from genetics

A portrait generator does not perform genetic testing. Even a real genetic analysis cannot produce a complete, certain image of a future person from two photographs. Appearance is shaped by many biological and environmental factors, while the image system works from visual inputs and patterns learned during development. The relationship between an adult's photograph and a synthetic face is therefore not a clinical or predictive result.

Do not use generated images to make decisions about parentage, medical risk, fertility, or family relationships. For questions about health or genetics, consult a qualified professional and use appropriate evidence. Presenting a synthetic face as proof could cause unnecessary anxiety or mislead others, especially if the picture is shared without its context.

## Share carefully, or not at all

Before sharing an output, confirm that everyone whose source photo was used agrees to that particular audience. Sharing with one person is different from publishing to a social network, where copying and redistribution can be difficult to control. Consider sending a lower-resolution copy or describing the exercise instead of posting the full image. Never attach a person's name or other identifying information unless they have agreed and it is necessary.

Make the synthetic nature visible in the caption and avoid implying that the image depicts a real child. If the picture is used as a fictional character reference, describe it that way. Keep the original source images separate from the output, and avoid packaging them together when a recipient only needs the finished illustration. If someone later withdraws permission, respond promptly and remove copies you control where practical.

## A short review checklist

Before finishing, ask four questions: did each adult agree to the upload and the intended audience; did you review the current privacy terms; is the image clearly identified as synthetic; and have you avoided presenting it as a prediction? If any answer is uncertain, pause before uploading or sharing. The safest useful outcome may be a private, clearly labeled creative image that is deleted when the conversation is over.

A responsible workflow does not require perfect technical knowledge. It requires clear expectations, respectful consent, attention to the service's actual terms, and an honest description of the result. Those habits help keep a lighthearted visual experiment separate from claims about real children or families.

## Copy-out review worksheet

Use this blank worksheet privately. Do not add source photographs or personal identifiers to a public repository.

| Review item | Decision to record |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Who is the intended audience and why is an illustration useful? |
| Consent | Did each adult agree to this specific upload and audience? |
| Privacy | What do the current service terms say about retention and deletion? |
| Framing | Is the synthetic image clearly labeled and free of predictive claims? |
| Retention | Which copies will be removed, by whom, and when? |

The table is a planning aid, not legal advice, a technical test, or a guarantee of a provider’s practices. Revisit the answers if the service changes or a new audience is proposed.
